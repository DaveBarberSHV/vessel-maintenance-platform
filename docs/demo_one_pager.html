Fathom — Polaris

An AI Technical Knowledge Assistant, Built for This Vessel

The Problem

A vessel's technical knowledge is scattered — manuals on thumb drives, drawings in binders, and the real, hard-won knowledge of experienced engineers that mostly never gets written down at all. Finding the right answer means searching through thousands of pages, or knowing exactly who to ask. When that engineer transfers or retires, the knowledge often goes with them.

What Fathom Does

Fathom is Polaris's own organized technical library, made instantly searchable. An engineer asks a plain-language question and gets a direct, cited answer — pulled from the vessel's actual manuals, drawings, and schematics, not a generic AI guess. Every answer shows exactly which document, revision, and page it came from, with the real page image alongside it.

Why It's Different

  • Verifiable, not a black box. Every answer cites its source down to the page — an engineer can check it in seconds.
  • Reads drawings, not just text. Wiring diagrams, schematics, and exploded parts views — content that's effectively been invisible in PDF form for years — are searchable and answerable, same as any manual page.
  • Knows this specific vessel. Fathom knows which exact model/variant of each system is actually installed on Polaris, so it never gives a generic "depends which version you have" answer.
  • Captures real crew knowledge. Engineer-submitted field notes are kept alongside manufacturer data, clearly labeled as crew experience — so hard-won institutional knowledge survives turnover instead of walking out the door.
  • Safety-forward. Relevant WARNING/CAUTION language from the source manual is surfaced automatically and verbatim, never paraphrased.
  • Works anywhere. Browser-based, no install, works on a phone at the equipment with tap-to-zoom on drawings.
  • A real trust commitment. Polaris's documents are used only to answer Polaris's own crew — never repurposed or shared elsewhere.

Proposed Path Forward — A No-Risk Trial

Phase 1 — Complete the library. We finish gathering any remaining vessel documentation (including TSMS and emergency response procedures) so Fathom's knowledge is as complete as possible before rollout.
Phase 2 — Deploy, with a site visit. We stand up the live system for Polaris's engineering crew and walk through it in person.
Phase 3 — Trial and measure. The crew uses Fathom in real day-to-day work for a trial period. We'll track concrete measures together — questions asked, engineer feedback, and time saved versus searching manually — so the value is evident, not just assumed.
The ask: if the trial shows real value, Polaris moves to a simple monthly subscription, cancel at any time — with the goal of a broader rollout across the fleet if it proves out.

What's Next on the Roadmap

Beyond the current system: a knowledge graph connecting interconnected shipboard systems for cross-system reasoning (e.g. tagout/lockout planning), and a curated library of general industry and regulatory reference material to fill gaps individual vessel manuals don't cover.
