# Fathom – Polaris: Rules of Behavior

For anyone with a login to this system. Read this once when you get
access, and again any time it's updated — then sign below.

---

## What this system holds

Fathom gives you access to Polaris's real technical library — manuals,
drawings, service data — plus Engineer Notes: real field knowledge
added by the crew. This is genuinely sensitive material: it includes
manufacturer documentation provided to us in confidence, and it will
increasingly include proprietary vessel and company information. Treat
it the same way you'd treat any other confidential company document.

## Your account

- **Never share your username or password with anyone** — including a
  coworker who's in a hurry. If someone else needs access, they get
  their own account.
- Choose a real password, not something guessable. If you think your
  account may have been compromised, say so immediately — see
  "Reporting a concern" below.
- Log out on a shared or public computer. On your own device, it's fine
  to stay logged in.

## Using the system

- Fathom is for real vessel maintenance and engineering work — asking
  about equipment, procedures, troubleshooting, and documenting real
  field knowledge via Engineer Notes.
- Every answer is generated from the actual documents in the system,
  with sources cited. If an answer seems wrong or doesn't match what
  you know from experience, trust your own judgment and flag it (a
  thumbs-down works, or just mention it directly) — this makes the
  system better for everyone.

## Engineer Notes carry real weight

If you're authorized to submit Engineer Notes, remember: these are
shown to whoever asks a related question, with your name and role
attached, and they can directly influence a real maintenance decision.
Only submit something you'd genuinely stand behind. If you're not sure
something's accurate, say so in the note itself rather than stating it
as fact.

## Keeping this data where it belongs

- Don't copy technical manual content, drawings, or Engineer Notes out
  of this system into somewhere less controlled (a personal email, a
  public forum, another AI tool) unless there's a real, specific reason
  to.
- Don't screenshot or export system content to share outside the
  people who are supposed to have access to it.

## Reporting a concern

If you notice anything that seems like a security problem — the system
behaving unexpectedly, a possible unauthorized access, or anything that
just seems off — **report it immediately.** Don't wait, and don't worry
about being wrong; a false alarm costs nothing, a missed real problem
could cost a lot.
